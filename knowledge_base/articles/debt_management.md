# Debt Management

## Good Debt vs. Bad Debt

### Good Debt
Debt used to acquire appreciating assets or increase earning power:
- **Mortgage**: Home usually appreciates; mortgage interest tax-deductible.
- **Student loans**: Invest in human capital (if ROI positive).
- **Business loans**: Fund income-generating activities.

### Bad Debt
Debt used to fund consumption, often at high interest rates:
- **Credit card debt**: 18–28% APR. Destroys wealth rapidly.
- **Payday loans**: Effective APR can exceed 400%.
- **Car loans for depreciating vehicles**: Paying interest on something losing value.
- **Buy-now-pay-later (BNPL)**: Can encourage overspending.

## The True Cost of Debt
$5,000 credit card balance at 22% APR, minimum payments only:
- Time to pay off: ~17 years
- Total interest paid: ~$6,200 (more than the original balance)

## Debt Payoff Strategies

### Debt Avalanche (Mathematically Optimal)
Pay minimums on all debts. Direct extra money to the highest interest rate debt first.
- Saves the most money in interest.
- Best mathematical outcome.
- May feel slow if the high-rate debt has a large balance.

### Debt Snowball (Psychologically Powerful)
Pay minimums on all debts. Direct extra money to the smallest balance debt first.
- Builds momentum with quick wins.
- Proven more effective at motivating people (Dave Ramsey method).
- Costs slightly more in interest.

### Debt Consolidation
Combine multiple high-interest debts into one lower-rate loan.
- Personal loan (8–15% APR) vs. credit cards (20%+ APR): clear win.
- Balance transfer card: 0% intro APR for 12–21 months. Pay off before rate jumps.
- Home equity loan/HELOC: Lowest rate but secured by your home — risky.

## Credit Card Debt Specifically
- Never carry a balance if avoidable — 20%+ interest is a guaranteed negative return.
- Pay in full every month.
- If you have card debt, it's almost always the right move to pay it off before investing.

## Student Loan Strategies
- **Federal loans**: Income-driven repayment (IDR), Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF), forbearance options. More flexibility.
- **Private loans**: Fixed terms, refinance to lower rate if credit improved.
- **Refinancing federal loans**: Converts to private — lose federal protections. Risky unless interest rate savings are very significant.

## Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)
DTI = Monthly debt payments / Gross monthly income.
- < 36%: Healthy. Lenders comfortable.
- 36–50%: Manageable but stretched.
- > 50%: Concerning. Difficulty getting new credit.

Mortgage lenders typically want DTI ≤ 43%.

## Emergency Fund vs. Paying Off Debt
Build a $1,000 mini emergency fund first. Then aggressively pay debt. Without any emergency fund, unexpected expenses go back on the credit card — undoing progress.

After high-interest debt is paid: Build full 3–6 month emergency fund before investing heavily.

## Debt Snowball Example
Debts: Credit card $800 @ 24%, Car loan $5,000 @ 8%, Student loan $15,000 @ 5%.
Extra $200/month available.

1. Pay minimums on car + student loan. Throw $200 + credit card minimum at $800 card. Pay off in ~3 months.
2. Roll freed-up payment to car loan. Pay off in ~18 months.
3. Roll to student loans. Pay off ~2 years early.
