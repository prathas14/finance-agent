# Alternative Investments

## What Are Alternative Investments?
Alternative investments are assets outside of traditional stocks, bonds, and cash. They include private equity, hedge funds, venture capital, private real estate, commodities, collectibles, and more. Previously accessible only to institutional investors and the ultra-wealthy, some alternatives are now available to retail investors.

## Why Consider Alternatives?
- **Diversification**: Low or negative correlation with public markets.
- **Return potential**: Private markets can offer higher returns (illiquidity premium).
- **Inflation protection**: Real assets (commodities, real estate) protect purchasing power.
- **Access**: Invest in companies before they go public.

## Major Alternative Asset Classes

### Private Equity
Investment in private (non-publicly traded) companies. Includes:
- **Buyout funds**: Take public companies private, restructure, resell.
- **Growth equity**: Invest in growth-stage private companies.
- **Venture capital (VC)**: Early-stage startup investing. Extremely high risk/return.

Historically: Private equity has outperformed public markets by 2-3% annually over long periods, though data has survivorship bias. Illiquid (10+ year lockups). High minimum investments ($250,000-$1M+). Now accessible via platforms like iCapital, Moonfare, and some Fidelity/Schwab platforms.

### Hedge Funds
Private funds using sophisticated strategies: short selling, leverage, derivatives, arbitrage. Aim for absolute returns (positive regardless of market direction).
- Historically: Average hedge fund has underperformed a simple 60/40 portfolio after fees.
- Fee: 2% of assets + 20% of profits ("2 and 20").
- Accessible to accredited investors ($1M+ net worth or $200k+ income).

### Real Estate (Private)
Direct property ownership or private real estate funds:
- **Crowdfunding platforms**: Fundrise, CrowdStreet — fractional real estate investing from $500.
- **Non-traded REITs**: Illiquid, higher potential return than public REITs.
- **Syndications**: Pool capital with other investors for specific properties.

### Infrastructure
Investments in roads, bridges, airports, utilities, pipelines. Long duration, inflation-linked returns. Stable cash flows. Most accessible via public infrastructure ETFs (IFRA, PAVE) or private funds.

### Collectibles and Tangible Assets
Art, wine, classic cars, sports cards, watches. Growing platforms: Masterworks (art), Rally (collectibles).
- Highly illiquid.
- No cash flows.
- Returns driven purely by buyer sentiment.
- Transaction costs are very high.
- Better as hobby than core investment strategy.

## Accredited Investor Requirements
To invest in most private alternatives in the US, you must be an "accredited investor":
- Net worth >$1 million (excluding primary residence), OR
- Annual income >$200,000 ($300,000 jointly) for past 2 years with expectation of continuation.
- OR hold certain financial licenses (Series 7, 65, 82).

## Allocation Guidance
Alternatives are typically 5-20% of a portfolio for those with access:
- Core portfolio: stocks + bonds.
- Satellite: Small allocation to alternatives for diversification.
- Avoid over-allocating — liquidity constraints can be painful in crises.
