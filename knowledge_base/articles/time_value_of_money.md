# Time Value of Money

## The Core Idea
A dollar today is worth more than a dollar in the future. This is because money available now can be invested to earn returns. The time value of money (TVM) is the foundation of all finance.

## Present Value (PV)
Present value answers: "What is a future sum worth today?"

Formula: PV = FV / (1 + r)^n

- FV = future value
- r = interest rate per period
- n = number of periods

Example: $10,000 received in 5 years at 6% discount rate = $10,000 / (1.06)^5 = $7,473 today.

## Future Value (FV)
Future value answers: "What will a sum today be worth later?"

Formula: FV = PV × (1 + r)^n

Example: $5,000 invested today at 7% for 10 years = $5,000 × (1.07)^10 = $9,836.

## Net Present Value (NPV)
NPV is used to evaluate investments by summing the present values of all future cash flows minus the initial investment. Positive NPV = good investment.

## Annuities
An annuity is a series of equal payments at regular intervals.

- **Ordinary annuity**: Payments at end of each period (most common).
- **Annuity due**: Payments at beginning of each period.

FV of annuity = PMT × [((1 + r)^n - 1) / r]

## Why TVM Matters for Personal Finance
- Explains why paying off high-interest debt early saves money
- Justifies investing early — more time means more compounding
- Used in mortgage, car loan, and retirement calculations
- Helps compare lump-sum vs. installment payment options
