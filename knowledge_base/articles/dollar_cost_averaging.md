# Dollar-Cost Averaging

## What Is Dollar-Cost Averaging?
Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) is the strategy of investing a fixed dollar amount at regular intervals (weekly, monthly, quarterly) regardless of the market price. Instead of trying to time the market, you invest consistently through all market conditions.

## How DCA Works
You invest $500/month in an S&P 500 index fund:

| Month | Price/Share | Shares Bought | Total Shares |
|-------|-------------|---------------|--------------|
| Jan | $100 | 5.0 | 5.0 |
| Feb | $80 | 6.25 | 11.25 |
| Mar | $90 | 5.56 | 16.81 |
| Apr | $110 | 4.55 | 21.36 |

Total invested: $2,000. Average purchase price: ~$94.55 (less than the simple average of $95). You bought more shares when prices were low, fewer when high.

## Benefits of DCA

### Eliminates Market Timing
No one consistently knows when the market is at the bottom. DCA sidesteps this problem entirely.

### Reduces Emotional Decision-Making
A pre-committed plan removes the temptation to pause investing during corrections (exactly when you should be buying more).

### Lower Average Cost
Buying more shares when prices are low naturally results in a lower average cost per share than the time-average price.

### Accessible
Works with any amount. $100/month invested for 30 years at 7% = ~$122,000. Starting with nothing.

### Automatic via Payroll / Auto-invest
Most 401(k)s and brokerage auto-invest features implement DCA automatically.

## DCA vs. Lump-Sum Investing
Research (including Vanguard's 2012 study) consistently shows that **lump-sum investing outperforms DCA roughly 2/3 of the time** — because markets rise more often than they fall, and cash not invested is earning less.

However:
- Most people don't have a lump sum available. DCA is the realistic alternative.
- DCA reduces the risk of terrible timing (investing your life savings the day before a 30% crash).
- DCA provides psychological comfort.

**Conclusion**: If you have a lump sum, the mathematically optimal choice is to invest it all immediately. If you're investing monthly income, DCA is natural and effective.

## Value Averaging: A DCA Variant
Instead of investing a fixed dollar amount, you invest whatever is needed to hit a target portfolio value each period.
- Portfolio target: grow by $500/month.
- If market is up $200, invest only $300.
- If market is down $100, invest $600.

More complex but can slightly improve returns over DCA. Requires holding cash reserves for "buy more" months.

## DCA for Volatile Assets
DCA is especially valuable for volatile assets like emerging market stocks or individual stocks, where timing risk is high and prices fluctuate significantly.

## The Best DCA Setup
1. Open a brokerage with automatic investment features (Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard).
2. Set up automatic monthly transfers from your bank.
3. Set up automatic investment into your chosen funds.
4. Never watch or adjust based on market conditions.
5. Increase amount whenever income rises.
