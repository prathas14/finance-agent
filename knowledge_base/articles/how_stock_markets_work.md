# How Stock Markets Work

## What Is a Stock Market?
A stock market is a marketplace where buyers and sellers trade shares of publicly listed companies. It serves two purposes: allowing companies to raise capital and giving investors a way to share in corporate profits.

## Primary vs. Secondary Markets
- **Primary market**: Where companies issue new shares to the public (IPO — Initial Public Offering). Money goes to the company.
- **Secondary market**: Where investors trade existing shares with each other. NYSE, NASDAQ are secondary markets. Money goes to the selling investor, not the company.

## Major US Exchanges
- **NYSE (New York Stock Exchange)**: Largest exchange by market cap. Traditional auction-based trading floor.
- **NASDAQ**: Electronic exchange. Home to many tech companies (Apple, Microsoft, Meta).
- **CBOE**: Options and volatility products.

## How a Trade Executes
1. Investor places an order through a broker.
2. Order is sent to the exchange or routed to a market maker.
3. A matching buy and sell order are paired.
4. Trade settles in T+1 (next business day for US stocks since 2024).

## Order Types
- **Market order**: Buy/sell immediately at current market price.
- **Limit order**: Buy/sell only at a specified price or better. May not execute.
- **Stop-loss order**: Automatically sells if price falls to a trigger level.
- **Stop-limit order**: Becomes a limit order when the stop price is hit.

## Bid-Ask Spread
- **Bid**: Highest price a buyer will pay.
- **Ask**: Lowest price a seller will accept.
- **Spread**: The difference. This is the market maker's profit and a transaction cost to investors.

For liquid stocks like Apple: spread may be $0.01. For illiquid small-caps: spread could be $0.50+.

## Market Indices
Indices track the performance of a group of stocks:
- **S&P 500**: 500 largest US companies. The most widely followed benchmark.
- **Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA)**: 30 large US companies. Price-weighted (older, less representative).
- **NASDAQ Composite**: All stocks on NASDAQ (~3,000+). Tech-heavy.
- **Russell 2000**: 2,000 small-cap US stocks.

## Market Hours
US markets are open Monday–Friday, 9:30 AM – 4:00 PM ET.
Pre-market (4–9:30 AM) and after-hours (4–8 PM) trading also occurs with lower liquidity.

## Market Participants
- **Retail investors**: Individual people investing personal money.
- **Institutional investors**: Mutual funds, pension funds, hedge funds, insurance companies.
- **Market makers**: Firms that provide liquidity by always quoting buy and sell prices.
- **High-frequency traders (HFT)**: Use algorithms to trade thousands of times per second.
