# Retirement Withdrawal Strategies

## The Challenge
Accumulating wealth for retirement is hard. Withdrawing it sustainably — so you don't run out of money — may be harder. The sequence of returns matters enormously: retiring into a bear market is far more damaging than retiring into a bull market.

## The 4% Rule
Based on the "Trinity Study" (1994). A retiree can withdraw 4% of their portfolio in year one, then adjust for inflation annually, and have a very high probability (~95%) of not running out of money over a 30-year retirement.

Example: $1,000,000 portfolio → withdraw $40,000/year, inflation-adjusted.

**Caveats:**
- Based on historical US market data (1926–1994).
- May be too aggressive given lower expected returns and longer lifespans today.
- Some advisors recommend 3–3.5% for extra safety.

## Sequence of Returns Risk
If you get poor returns early in retirement, you sell more shares at low prices to fund living expenses — leaving fewer shares to recover when markets rebound.

Example: Two retirees both average 6% annually, but one gets bad years first vs. good years first. The one with bad early years may run out of money while the other thrives.

**Mitigation strategies:**
- Keep 1-2 years of expenses in cash (cash buffer / bucket strategy).
- Maintain a bond allocation as a stable withdrawal source in downturns.
- Flexible spending — reduce withdrawals in bad years.

## The Bucket Strategy
Divide portfolio into time-based buckets:
- **Bucket 1 (Years 1–2)**: Cash, money market. No market risk.
- **Bucket 2 (Years 3–10)**: Bonds, stable assets. Refills Bucket 1.
- **Bucket 3 (Years 10+)**: Stocks for growth. Refills Bucket 2 over time.

Provides psychological comfort and reduces forced selling in downturns.

## Tax-Efficient Withdrawal Order
General guidance:
1. Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) first — mandatory.
2. Taxable brokerage accounts — take advantage of lower capital gains rates.
3. Traditional IRA / 401(k) — taxed as income.
4. Roth IRA — last resort (tax-free, no RMDs).

This order minimizes lifetime taxes for most retirees.

## Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)
The IRS requires withdrawals from Traditional IRAs and 401(k)s starting at age 73 (SECURE 2.0 Act, 2023). Miss an RMD and face a 25% excise tax on the amount not withdrawn.

RMD = Account balance (Dec 31 prior year) / IRS life expectancy factor

## Social Security Optimization
- Claim at 62: Permanently reduced benefit (~30% less).
- Claim at 67 (full retirement age): Standard benefit.
- Claim at 70: Maximum benefit (~32% more than full retirement age).

Delaying is often the better choice if healthy. Each year after 62 increases benefit by 6-8%.

## Safe Withdrawal in Low-Return Environments
When bond yields are low and stock valuations are high, the 4% rule may be too generous. Alternatives:
- **Dynamic withdrawal**: Adjust spending based on portfolio performance.
- **Guardrails strategy**: Set upper/lower spending bounds tied to portfolio value.
- **Annuity floor**: Use a portion of savings to buy a lifetime income annuity, covering essential expenses.
