# Compound Interest: A Deep Dive

## Simple vs. Compound Interest
- **Simple interest**: Calculated only on the principal. $1,000 at 5% for 3 years = $150 total interest.
- **Compound interest**: Calculated on principal PLUS accumulated interest. The same $1,000 at 5% compounded annually for 3 years = $157.63.

The difference seems small early on but becomes enormous over decades.

## Compounding Frequency
The more frequently interest compounds, the faster money grows.

| Frequency | $10,000 at 6% for 20 years |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| Annual | $32,071 |
| Quarterly | $32,620 |
| Monthly | $32,776 |
| Daily | $33,199 |

## The Compound Interest Formula
A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t)

- A = final amount
- P = principal
- r = annual interest rate (decimal)
- n = compounding periods per year
- t = time in years

## The Rule of 72
Divide 72 by the annual return to estimate years to double your money.
- 6% return: 72/6 = 12 years to double
- 9% return: 72/9 = 8 years to double
- 12% return: 72/12 = 6 years to double

## Starting Early: The Most Powerful Variable

Example — two investors, both earn 7% annually:
- **Alex** invests $5,000/year from age 25–35 (10 years, $50,000 total) then stops.
- **Jordan** invests $5,000/year from age 35–65 (30 years, $150,000 total).

At age 65: Alex has ~$602,000. Jordan has ~$472,000.
Alex invested 1/3 the money but has MORE — purely because of time.

## Compound Interest on Debt
Compounding works against you with debt. A $5,000 credit card balance at 22% APR compounded monthly:
- After 1 year (minimum payments only): balance barely decreases
- Total interest paid over life: can exceed the original balance

## Continuous Compounding
The theoretical maximum compounding frequency uses the formula:
A = P × e^(r×t)

Where e ≈ 2.718. Used in advanced finance and options pricing.
