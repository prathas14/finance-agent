# Bond Types

## Government Bonds

### US Treasury Securities
Issued by the US federal government. Considered the safest investment in the world (backed by the "full faith and credit" of the US government).

- **Treasury Bills (T-Bills)**: Maturity < 1 year. Sold at discount, no coupon.
- **Treasury Notes (T-Notes)**: Maturity 2–10 years. Pay semi-annual coupons.
- **Treasury Bonds (T-Bonds)**: Maturity 20–30 years. Pay semi-annual coupons.
- **TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities)**: Principal adjusts with CPI. Protects against inflation.
- **I-Bonds**: Savings bonds with fixed + inflation-adjusted rate. Max $10,000/year per person. Great for emergency fund.

### Municipal Bonds (Munis)
Issued by state and local governments to fund infrastructure. Key benefit: interest is usually exempt from federal income tax and often state tax too. Attractive to high-income investors in high tax brackets.

- **General obligation bonds**: Backed by the issuer's taxing power.
- **Revenue bonds**: Backed by revenue from a specific project (toll road, airport).

## Corporate Bonds

Issued by companies to raise capital for operations or expansion.

- **Investment-grade**: Rated BBB- or higher (S&P). Lower yield, lower risk. Issued by stable large companies.
- **High-yield (junk bonds)**: Rated below BBB-. Higher yield to compensate for higher default risk.

## Agency Bonds
Issued by US government-sponsored entities (GSEs): Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, Federal Home Loan Banks. Slightly higher yield than Treasuries. Implicitly (not explicitly) guaranteed by government.

## International Bonds
- **Sovereign bonds**: Government bonds from other countries (UK Gilts, German Bunds, Japanese JGBs).
- **Eurobonds**: Bonds issued in a currency different from the issuer's home currency.
- **Emerging market bonds**: Higher yield but currency and political risk.

## Convertible Bonds
Corporate bonds that can be converted into a set number of shares at the bondholder's option. Hybrid security — bond downside protection with equity upside.

## Zero-Coupon Bonds
Pay no periodic interest. Issued at deep discount, repay face value at maturity. The difference is the investor's return. Tax treatment: "phantom income" — taxed annually even though no cash is received.
