# Dividends

## What Are Dividends?
Dividends are payments a company makes to shareholders from its profits. They represent a share of the company's earnings returned directly to investors, typically in cash.

## Key Dividend Metrics
- **Dividend yield**: Annual dividend per share / stock price. A $2/year dividend on a $40 stock = 5% yield.
- **Dividend per share (DPS)**: Total dividends paid / shares outstanding.
- **Payout ratio**: Dividends paid / net earnings. 40-60% is considered sustainable. Above 80% may be unsustainable.
- **Dividend growth rate**: How fast the dividend increases year-over-year.

## Important Dates
- **Declaration date**: Board announces the dividend.
- **Ex-dividend date**: You must own shares BEFORE this date to receive the dividend.
- **Record date**: Company checks its records to identify shareholders.
- **Payment date**: Dividend is paid.

## Types of Dividends
- **Cash dividends**: Most common. Direct cash payment.
- **Stock dividends**: Additional shares instead of cash. Dilutes existing shares.
- **Special dividends**: One-time large payment, often after an asset sale.
- **Return of capital**: Returns part of your own investment. Not taxed as income immediately but reduces cost basis.

## Dividend Reinvestment (DRIP)
Automatically reinvest dividends to buy more shares. Accelerates compounding significantly over time. Most brokers offer DRIP for free.

## Dividend Aristocrats and Kings
- **Dividend Aristocrats**: S&P 500 companies that have raised dividends for 25+ consecutive years (e.g. Johnson & Johnson, Coca-Cola, Procter & Gamble).
- **Dividend Kings**: Companies with 50+ consecutive years of dividend increases.

## Taxation of Dividends
- **Qualified dividends**: Taxed at lower capital gains rates (0%, 15%, or 20%) if you hold the stock for 60+ days.
- **Ordinary dividends**: Taxed as regular income. Applies to REITs and some foreign stocks.

## High Yield vs. Dividend Growth
- **High-yield strategy**: Focus on current income. Higher risk of dividend cuts.
- **Dividend growth strategy**: Focus on companies consistently growing dividends. Builds income over time.

## Are Dividends "Free Money"?
No. When a stock pays a dividend, the stock price typically drops by the dividend amount on the ex-date. You're effectively receiving part of the company's value back. Total return = price appreciation + dividends received.
