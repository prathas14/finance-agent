# ETF Types

## What Is an ETF?
An ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund) is a basket of securities that trades on a stock exchange. It combines the diversification of a mutual fund with the trading flexibility of a stock.

## By Asset Class

### Equity ETFs
Track stock indices or sectors.
- **Broad market**: VTI (Total US Market), ITOT, SCHB
- **S&P 500**: SPY, IVV, VOO — most popular ETFs globally
- **International developed**: VEA, EFA
- **Emerging markets**: VWO, EEM
- **Sector ETFs**: XLK (technology), XLF (financials), XLE (energy), XLV (healthcare)

### Bond ETFs
- **Total bond market**: BND, AGG
- **Treasuries**: GOVT, SHY (short-term), TLT (long-term 20+ year)
- **Corporate bonds**: LQD (investment grade), HYG (high yield)
- **Inflation-protected**: TIP, VTIP

### Commodity ETFs
- **Gold**: GLD, IAU
- **Silver**: SLV
- **Oil**: USO
- **Broad commodities**: PDBC, DJP

### Real Estate ETFs (REITs)
- VNQ (Vanguard Real Estate): Broad US REIT exposure
- REET: Global REIT ETF

### Multi-Asset ETFs
- **Balanced funds**: AOR (60/40 stocks/bonds), AOM (40/60)
- **All-in-one**: AOA (80% stocks)

## By Strategy

### Passive (Index) ETFs
Track a predetermined index. No active stock picking. Low expense ratios (often 0.03–0.20%). Account for ~70% of ETF assets.

### Active ETFs
Portfolio managers make investment decisions. Higher expense ratios. Growing category — ARK Innovation (ARKK) is a famous (controversial) example.

### Smart Beta / Factor ETFs
Track rules-based indices targeting specific factors:
- Value: VTV, IVE
- Growth: VUG, IVW
- Dividend: VYM, DGRO, SCHD
- Low volatility: USMV, SPLV
- Momentum: MTUM

### Thematic ETFs
Target specific investment themes: clean energy (ICLN), cybersecurity (HACK), cloud computing (SKYY), genomics (ARKG). Higher expense ratios and concentration risk.

### Leveraged and Inverse ETFs
- **Leveraged**: 2x or 3x daily returns. TQQQ (3x Nasdaq), SPXL (3x S&P 500). Decay rapidly over time — only for very short-term trading.
- **Inverse**: Profit when index falls. SQQQ (-3x Nasdaq). Not buy-and-hold vehicles.

## Key Metrics for Comparing ETFs
- **Expense ratio**: Annual cost. Index ETFs typically 0.03–0.20%.
- **Assets under management (AUM)**: Larger funds are more liquid and unlikely to close.
- **Bid-ask spread**: Lower is better. Wide spreads increase trading costs.
- **Tracking error**: How closely the ETF follows its index.
- **Dividend yield and distribution schedule**: Monthly, quarterly, or annually.
