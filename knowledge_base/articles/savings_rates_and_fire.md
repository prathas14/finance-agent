# Savings Rates and the FIRE Movement

## The Power of Savings Rate
Your savings rate — the percentage of income you save and invest — is the single most powerful lever in building wealth and determining when you can retire.

Savings rate = (Income - Spending) / Income × 100

## Savings Rate and Years to Retirement
Assuming 5% real investment return and you start from zero:

| Savings Rate | Years to Retirement |
|-------------|-------------------|
| 5% | ~66 years |
| 10% | ~51 years |
| 20% | ~37 years |
| 30% | ~28 years |
| 50% | ~17 years |
| 70% | ~9 years |
| 90% | ~3 years |

This assumes you can live on your current spending indefinitely and follow the 4% withdrawal rule.

## What Is FIRE?
FIRE stands for **Financial Independence, Retire Early**. A movement of people optimising their finances to reach financial independence — the point where investment income covers living expenses — as quickly as possible.

Financial independence ≠ quitting work. It means having the choice.

## Your FI Number
The total invested assets you need to be financially independent.
FI number = Annual expenses × 25 (using the 4% rule)

Annual spending of $40,000 → FI number = $1,000,000.
Annual spending of $80,000 → FI number = $2,000,000.

Reducing spending does double work: needs less invested AND saves faster.

## FIRE Variants

### Lean FIRE
Retire very early with minimal spending budget. Often <$40,000/year. High frugality. Maximum flexibility required.

### Fat FIRE
Financial independence with a generous spending budget ($100,000+/year). Requires larger portfolio ($2.5M+) but not extreme frugality.

### Barista FIRE
Retire from demanding career but do part-time work for healthcare benefits and supplemental income. Portfolio covers most expenses.

### Coast FIRE
Save enough that, left alone, investments will grow to fund retirement at normal age — even if you never contribute another dollar. Then "coast" with lighter work.

## Optimising Savings Rate
- **Reduce the big 3**: Housing, transportation, food.
- **Avoid lifestyle inflation**: When income rises, save the increase.
- **Tax optimisation**: Max 401k, IRA, HSA — tax savings directly increase savings rate.
- **Earn more**: Side income, career growth, entrepreneurship — no ceiling on income.

## Criticisms of FIRE
- Aggressive savings rates may require sacrificing current quality of life.
- Sequence of returns risk is heightened for very long (50-60 year) retirements.
- Healthcare costs are a major X factor, especially before Medicare at 65.
- Re-entry into workforce may be difficult after extended absence.
- 4% rule was designed for 30-year retirements, not 50-60 years.

## The Middle Path
You don't have to retire at 35 to benefit from FIRE principles. Reaching financial independence at 50 instead of 65 gives you 15 extra years of freedom. Many FIRE adherents still work — just on their own terms.
