# Robo-Advisors

## What Is a Robo-Advisor?
A robo-advisor is an automated digital investment platform that builds and manages a diversified portfolio for you based on your risk tolerance and goals. It uses algorithms rather than human advisors to make investment decisions.

## How They Work
1. Answer questions about risk tolerance, time horizon, and financial goals.
2. Algorithm recommends a portfolio (typically ETFs across asset classes).
3. You fund the account.
4. Robo-advisor automatically rebalances and often tax-loss harvests.
5. You pay a management fee.

## Major Robo-Advisors

### Betterment
- Management fee: 0.25%/year.
- Automatic tax-loss harvesting (daily).
- SRI (socially responsible) options.
- No minimum.
- Premium tier ($100,000+): 0.40% for access to human advisors.

### Wealthfront
- Management fee: 0.25%/year.
- Tax-loss harvesting, direct indexing at higher balances.
- Financial planning tools.
- $500 minimum.
- 529 account management.

### Schwab Intelligent Portfolios
- 0% management fee (but holds ~8% cash — implicit drag).
- $5,000 minimum.
- Premium: $30/month for unlimited advisor access.

### Fidelity Go
- 0% fee up to $25,000; 0.35% above.
- Very low minimum.
- No tax-loss harvesting.

### Vanguard Digital Advisor
- ~0.20% fee.
- Targets full financial plan, not just investments.

## Robo-Advisor vs. DIY Indexing
| Feature | Robo-Advisor | DIY Three-Fund Portfolio |
|---------|-------------|------------------------|
| Cost | 0.25-0.35% | ~0.05-0.10% (just ETF fees) |
| Tax-loss harvesting | Automated | Manual |
| Rebalancing | Automated | Annual, manual |
| Simplicity | Very high | Moderate |
| Control | Less | More |

For a $100,000 portfolio, robo-advisor costs ~$250/year more than DIY. That covers: automatic rebalancing + daily tax-loss harvesting (can save more than the fee for high earners).

## Who Should Use a Robo-Advisor?
Ideal for:
- Investors who want automation and simplicity.
- Those who won't manually rebalance.
- Investors in high tax brackets who benefit from automated tax-loss harvesting.
- People just starting and not yet comfortable with DIY investing.

May be overkill or too expensive for:
- Very simple portfolios (one or two index funds).
- Those comfortable with annual manual rebalancing.
- Very low-fee, low-account-balance situations (fee drag is proportionally larger on small accounts).

## Limitations
- Less flexibility than DIY or human advisors.
- Generic portfolios may not match your specific situation (tax situation, other accounts).
- No tax coordination across accounts (most don't see your 401k at another institution).
- Human advisors better for complex situations (business ownership, divorce, inheritance).
