# 401(k) Plans

## What Is a 401(k)?
A 401(k) is an employer-sponsored retirement savings plan that offers significant tax advantages. Named after Section 401(k) of the IRS tax code. It's the most common retirement savings vehicle in the US.

## How It Works
1. You elect a percentage of your paycheck to contribute pre-tax.
2. Contributions reduce your taxable income for the year.
3. Money grows tax-deferred — no taxes on dividends, interest, or capital gains while invested.
4. Withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income.

## Contribution Limits (2024)
- Employee contribution limit: $23,000/year
- Catch-up contribution (age 50+): Additional $7,500/year
- Total limit including employer contributions: $69,000/year

## Employer Match
Many employers match a portion of contributions — effectively free money.

Common match: "100% match up to 3% of salary" — if you earn $60,000 and contribute 3% ($1,800), employer adds $1,800.

**Always contribute enough to get the full employer match.** This is a guaranteed 100% return on that portion of your investment.

## Traditional 401(k) vs. Roth 401(k)
| Feature | Traditional 401(k) | Roth 401(k) |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|
| Contributions | Pre-tax | After-tax |
| Tax now | Reduced | None |
| Growth | Tax-deferred | Tax-free |
| Withdrawals | Taxed as income | Tax-free |
| Best if | Expect lower tax rate in retirement | Expect higher tax rate in retirement |

## Vesting Schedule
Employer match may have a vesting schedule — you only "own" the match after working a certain period.
- **Cliff vesting**: 0% until 3 years, then 100%.
- **Graded vesting**: 20% per year over 5 years.
- Immediate vesting is most employee-friendly.

## Investment Options
401(k) plans offer a menu of funds (typically 10-30 options). Look for:
- Low expense ratio index funds
- Target-date funds as a simple default
- Avoid high-fee actively managed funds

## Withdrawals
- Age 59½: Can withdraw without penalty.
- Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs): Must begin at age 73.
- Early withdrawal (before 59½): 10% penalty + income taxes.
- Exceptions: Disability, death, substantially equal periodic payments (SEPP), hardship withdrawal.

## Loans from 401(k)
Most plans allow borrowing up to 50% of vested balance (max $50,000). Must repay with interest. If you leave the job, loan may become immediately due. Risky — avoid if possible.

## Rollovers
When changing jobs:
- Roll over to new employer's 401(k)
- Roll over to an IRA (more investment options, more control)
- Leave in old employer's plan (if good options/fees)
- Cash out (avoid — triggers taxes and 10% penalty)
