# Commodities

## What Are Commodities?
Commodities are raw materials or primary agricultural products that can be bought and sold. Unlike stocks or bonds, they represent physical goods. They are interchangeable with other goods of the same type ("fungible") — one barrel of West Texas crude is equivalent to another.

## Major Commodity Categories

### Energy
- Crude oil (WTI, Brent)
- Natural gas
- Gasoline, heating oil
- Uranium (nuclear energy)

### Metals
- **Precious metals**: Gold, silver, platinum, palladium
- **Industrial metals**: Copper, aluminum, iron ore, nickel, zinc

### Agricultural
- Grains: Wheat, corn, soybeans, rice
- Soft commodities: Coffee, cocoa, sugar, cotton
- Livestock: Cattle, hogs

## Why Invest in Commodities?

### Inflation Hedge
Commodity prices generally rise with inflation — they ARE the inputs that drive inflation. Gold and broad commodity indices have historically maintained purchasing power.

### Diversification
Low or negative correlation with stocks and bonds. Tend to rise when stocks and bonds struggle (stagflation, supply shocks). Adding 5-10% commodities to a portfolio can reduce overall volatility.

### Supply/Demand Fundamentals
Driven by real-world supply and demand, not just financial sentiment. Geopolitical events, weather, and production changes create price movements independent of stock market.

## How to Invest in Commodities

### Commodity ETFs and ETNs
- **GLD / IAU**: Gold ETFs backed by physical gold.
- **PDBC**: Invesco Optimum Yield Diversified Commodity Strategy ETF.
- **DBC**: Invesco DB Commodity Index Tracking Fund.
- **CPER**: Copper ETF.

### Mining and Energy Stocks
Companies that extract commodities (Freeport-McMoRan for copper, ExxonMobil for oil). Provides commodity exposure with additional company risk.

### Futures Contracts
Direct exposure to commodity prices. Used by professional investors and hedgers. Highly complex — contango and backwardation affect returns significantly. Not suitable for most retail investors.

### REITs (Timber, Agriculture)
Farmland REITs (Farmland Partners) or timber REITs provide commodity exposure with income.

## Gold: Special Considerations
Gold is the most common commodity holding for individual investors:
- No cash flows (no dividends, no earnings).
- Value is based entirely on perception of store of value.
- Historically holds value over centuries but volatile decade-to-decade.
- Performs well during financial stress and when real interest rates are negative.
- Appropriate allocation: 5-10% for those who want it; 0% is also defensible.

## Contango and Backwardation
For commodity futures ETFs (not physical):
- **Contango**: Near-term futures cheaper than far-term → rolling futures forward costs money. Drag on returns. Affects oil ETFs significantly.
- **Backwardation**: Near-term futures more expensive → rolling futures forward earns money.

Physical commodity ETFs (like gold's GLD) avoid this issue entirely.
