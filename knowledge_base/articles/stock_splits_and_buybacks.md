# Stock Splits and Share Buybacks

## Stock Splits

### What Is a Stock Split?
A stock split increases the number of shares while proportionally reducing the price per share. Total market value is unchanged.

Example — 2-for-1 split: You own 100 shares at $200 each ($20,000 total). After split: 200 shares at $100 each ($20,000 total).

### Why Companies Split Stocks
- Make shares more accessible to retail investors (lower price per share).
- Improve liquidity and trading volume.
- Perceived as positive signal about management confidence.

### Common Split Ratios
- 2-for-1: Most common.
- 3-for-1, 4-for-1, 10-for-1: Used when share price has risen dramatically (e.g. Apple, Tesla, NVIDIA).

### Reverse Stock Split
The opposite: fewer shares at a higher price. Companies use this to avoid being delisted (exchanges require minimum share prices). Often a negative signal — struggling companies do this. 1-for-10 reverse split: 100 shares at $0.50 → 10 shares at $5.00.

## Share Buybacks (Repurchases)

### What Is a Buyback?
A company uses its cash to buy back its own shares from the open market, reducing the total shares outstanding.

### Effect on Shareholders
- Earnings per share (EPS) increases (same earnings, fewer shares).
- Remaining shareholders own a larger percentage of the company.
- Can support the stock price.

### Buybacks vs. Dividends
Both return cash to shareholders, but:
| | Buybacks | Dividends |
|--|---------|-----------|
| Flexibility | Can be stopped easily | Cutting dividend signals distress |
| Tax | Capital gains (deferred) | Taxed when received |
| Signal | Management thinks stock is undervalued | Consistent income commitment |

### Criticism of Buybacks
- Can be used to boost EPS optically without real earnings growth.
- Companies may repurchase at high prices (destroying value).
- Critics argue cash should be invested in the business or workers.

### Scale
US companies have bought back trillions in stock. Apple alone repurchased over $600 billion in stock between 2012 and 2023 — the largest buyback program in corporate history.
