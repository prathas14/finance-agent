# HSA and FSA: Healthcare Savings Accounts

## Health Savings Account (HSA)

### What Is an HSA?
A Health Savings Account is a tax-advantaged account for people enrolled in a High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP). Often called the "triple tax advantage" account.

### The Triple Tax Advantage
1. **Contributions are tax-deductible** (or pre-tax if through payroll).
2. **Growth is tax-free** — invest in index funds and dividends/gains aren't taxed.
3. **Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free**.

No other account offers all three benefits simultaneously.

### 2024 Contribution Limits
- Individual coverage: $4,150/year
- Family coverage: $8,300/year
- Catch-up (age 55+): Additional $1,000/year

### HDHP Requirements (2024)
To be eligible for an HSA, your health plan must have:
- Minimum deductible: $1,600 (individual) / $3,200 (family)
- Maximum out-of-pocket: $8,050 (individual) / $16,100 (family)

### Qualified Medical Expenses
Doctor visits, prescriptions, dental care, vision, medical equipment, mental health, and more. See IRS Publication 502 for the full list.

### HSA as a Retirement Account
After age 65, you can withdraw for any reason (not just medical) — taxed as ordinary income, like a Traditional IRA. Before 65, non-medical withdrawals incur income tax + 20% penalty.

**Strategy**: Pay medical expenses out-of-pocket now (keeping receipts), invest HSA contributions, and reimburse yourself years later for old medical expenses — tax-free. There's no time limit on reimbursements.

### HSA Portability
Unlike FSAs, HSA funds roll over indefinitely. The account belongs to you — not your employer. You keep it if you change jobs or retire.

## Flexible Spending Account (FSA)

### What Is an FSA?
An FSA is an employer-sponsored benefit that lets you set aside pre-tax dollars for healthcare expenses. More widely available than HSAs (doesn't require HDHP).

### Types of FSAs
- **Healthcare FSA**: Medical, dental, vision expenses.
- **Dependent Care FSA**: Childcare, elder care while you work.

### 2024 Contribution Limits
- Healthcare FSA: $3,200/year
- Dependent Care FSA: $5,000/year ($2,500 if married filing separately)

### Use-It-Or-Lose-It Rule
The major drawback: unused FSA funds are forfeited at year-end (unlike HSA). Some plans offer:
- $640 rollover to next year, OR
- 2.5-month grace period to spend remaining funds.

### FSA vs. HSA — Key Differences
| Feature | HSA | FSA |
|---------|-----|-----|
| HDHP required | Yes | No |
| Rollover | Full | Limited or none |
| Portability | Yes | No (employer-tied) |
| Investment option | Yes | Generally no |
| Contribution | You control | Set at enrollment |
| Triple tax benefit | Yes | Two benefits (in/out) |

## Bottom Line
If you're healthy and can afford a higher-deductible plan, an HSA is arguably the best savings vehicle available in the US tax code. Maximize it, invest it, and treat it as a stealth retirement account for healthcare costs.
