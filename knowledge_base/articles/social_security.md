# Social Security

## What Is Social Security?
Social Security is a federal program providing retirement income, disability insurance (SSDI), and survivor benefits. Funded by payroll taxes (FICA): 6.2% from employees and 6.2% from employers on wages up to $168,600 (2024). Self-employed pay both portions (12.4%).

## How Benefits Are Calculated
Benefits are based on your 35 highest-earning years, indexed for inflation. The formula heavily favors lower earners (progressive replacement rate).

**Primary Insurance Amount (PIA)**: The monthly benefit at your full retirement age.

## Full Retirement Age (FRA)
The age at which you receive 100% of your PIA:
- Born 1943–1954: Age 66
- Born 1955–1959: 66 + 2 months for each year
- Born 1960 or later: Age 67

## When to Claim: The Critical Decision

### Claim at 62 (Earliest)
Benefit reduced permanently: ~30% below FRA amount for those with FRA of 67. Receive benefits longer but each check is smaller.

### Claim at FRA
Receive 100% of PIA.

### Delay to 70 (Maximum)
Benefit increases 8% per year after FRA. At 70 vs. 67: ~24% higher monthly benefit. No benefit to delaying past 70.

## Break-Even Analysis
Each extra year of delay requires a "break-even" point to justify it.

Claiming at 67 vs. 62: Receive ~$700/month more at 67. Missed ~$1,200/month for 5 years ($72,000). Break-even: ~100 months (~age 75) after claiming at 67.

If you expect to live past the break-even point, delaying is better. Average life expectancy for someone reaching 65 today: ~84 (men) / ~87 (women).

## Spousal Benefits
A spouse who didn't work (or earned little) can receive up to 50% of the higher earner's FRA benefit. The higher earner should generally delay to maximize the spousal benefit and survivor benefit.

## Survivor Benefits
A surviving spouse can claim the deceased spouse's benefit if it's higher than their own. This makes delay strategy even more valuable for the higher earner in a couple — the surviving spouse inherits the larger benefit.

## Working While Collecting Social Security
Before FRA: Benefits reduced $1 for every $2 earned above $22,320 (2024). Not lost — credits added back at FRA.
At/after FRA: No earnings limit. Work and collect full benefits.

## Social Security Taxation
Up to 85% of Social Security benefits may be taxable depending on your "combined income":
- Combined income = AGI + non-taxable interest + 50% of SS benefits
- Single filer: <$25,000 → 0% taxable; $25,000-$34,000 → 50%; >$34,000 → 85%

## Long-Term Solvency
Social Security faces a funding shortfall. Current projections: trust fund depleted around 2033, at which point ongoing payroll taxes fund only ~77% of promised benefits. Congress has historically addressed this before reaching the point of cuts.
