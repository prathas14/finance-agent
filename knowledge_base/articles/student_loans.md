# Student Loans

## The US Student Loan Landscape
Total US student loan debt exceeds $1.7 trillion. The average borrower owes ~$37,000. For graduate/professional degrees, six-figure debt is common. Understanding your loans is essential to managing them effectively.

## Federal vs. Private Loans

### Federal Loans
Issued by the US Department of Education. Offer income-driven repayment, forgiveness programs, deferment, and forbearance. Always prefer federal loans before considering private.

Types:
- **Direct Subsidised Loans**: Need-based. Government pays interest during school and grace period.
- **Direct Unsubsidised Loans**: Not need-based. Interest accrues immediately.
- **Direct PLUS Loans**: For graduate students or parents. Higher rates, credit check required.
- **Perkins Loans**: Discontinued but many borrowers still repay these.

### Private Loans
Issued by banks, credit unions, or online lenders. Variable or fixed rates. No income-driven repayment. No forgiveness. No federal protections. Use only after exhausting federal loan eligibility.

## Federal Repayment Plans

### Standard (10-Year)
Fixed monthly payments over 10 years. Pays off fastest, least total interest. Default plan.

### Extended
Payments over 25 years. Lower monthly payment, more total interest.

### Graduated
Payments start low, increase every 2 years. For those expecting income growth.

### Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) Plans
Payments based on income and family size. After 20–25 years of payments, remaining balance forgiven (forgiven amount currently taxable as income).

Key IDR plans:
- **SAVE (Saving on a Valuable Education)**: Newest plan. 5-10% of discretionary income. Shortest forgiveness timeline for undergrad loans (10 years for balances <$12,000).
- **IBR (Income-Based Repayment)**: 10-15% of discretionary income.
- **PAYE**: 10% of discretionary income. Forgiveness at 20 years.

## Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF)
Work full-time for a qualifying employer (government, 501(c)(3) nonprofits) + make 120 qualifying payments (10 years) under an IDR plan = remaining balance forgiven, **tax-free**.

Best deal in student loan management for qualifying borrowers. Employment includes: teachers, nurses, public defenders, nonprofit workers, many healthcare workers.

Use the PSLF Help Tool on studentaid.gov to verify employer eligibility.

## Refinancing
Replacing federal or private loans with a new private loan, typically at a lower rate.

**When it makes sense**:
- High income, stable employment, debt is reasonable relative to income.
- Not pursuing PSLF or IDR forgiveness.
- Private loans with high rates.

**When to avoid**:
- If pursuing PSLF — refinancing makes you ineligible (converts to private loan).
- If income is uncertain — lose IDR protections.
- If you have federal PLUS loans at high rates but might need income protection.

## The ROI Question
Student debt is only "good debt" if the education increases your earning power more than the debt costs.

**Rule of thumb**: Total undergraduate debt should not exceed your expected first-year salary.
- Education degree, $40,000 debt: reasonable.
- Law degree, $200,000 debt → entry-level salary $70,000: problematic.
- Computer science, $80,000 debt → $110,000 first job: fine.

## Repayment Strategy
- Always pay at least the minimum — interest capitalises (adds to principal) in many plans if you don't.
- Make PSLF-qualifying payments even if the balance isn't growing — time in qualifying repayment counts.
- If not pursuing forgiveness, pay extra toward highest-rate loans.
- Consider employer student loan repayment benefits — many companies now offer this tax-free.
