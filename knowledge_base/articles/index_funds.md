# Index Funds

## What Is an Index Fund?
An index fund is a fund (mutual fund or ETF) that tracks a market index — a predefined list of securities — rather than having a manager actively pick stocks. It holds every stock in the index (or a representative sample) in proportion to their weight.

## What Is a Market Index?
An index is a measuring stick — a hypothetical portfolio representing a segment of the market.
- **S&P 500**: 500 largest US companies by market cap. ~80% of total US market value.
- **Total US Market**: ~4,000 companies including small and mid-cap.
- **MSCI All Country World Index (ACWI)**: ~2,900 companies in 47 countries.

## Why Index Funds Dominate
Jack Bogle founded Vanguard and launched the first retail index fund in 1976. The investing world resisted — calling it "Bogle's folly." Decades of data proved him right.

**The arithmetic of active management**: All investors collectively own the market. Some beat it, some lag. After fees, the average active investor must underperform by exactly the average cost of active management. Index funds win by minimizing costs.

## Evidence Against Active Management
- S&P SPIVA Report: Over 15 years, ~90% of US large-cap active funds underperform the S&P 500.
- The few that outperform are difficult to identify in advance — last decade's winners rarely lead the next.
- Even professional pension fund managers with full-time analysts and superior data cannot consistently beat indexes.

## Market-Cap vs. Equal-Weight Indexing
Most indexes are **market-cap weighted**: larger companies have larger weight. The S&P 500 has Apple, Microsoft, and Amazon comprising 15%+ of the index.

**Equal-weight**: Each of 500 companies gets 0.2%. More small-cap exposure, higher turnover, typically higher expense ratios.

## Total Market vs. S&P 500
Both are excellent. Total market adds ~3,500 small and mid-cap US stocks (approximately 20% of portfolio vs. pure S&P 500). Small-caps have historically offered a risk premium over large-caps. The difference is small in practice.

## The Three-Fund Portfolio
The simplest complete portfolio using index funds:
1. **US Total Stock Market** (VTI): Domestic equity.
2. **International Total Stock Market** (VXUS): Global diversification.
3. **US Total Bond Market** (BND): Stability and income.

Adjust ratios: aggressive investor: 60/30/10. Conservative: 40/20/40. Rebalance annually.

## Criticisms of Index Investing
- **Concentration risk**: Mega-cap tech dominates market-cap indexes.
- **No downside protection**: Index funds fall with the market.
- **Valuation-blind**: Buys expensive and cheap stocks equally.
- **Ownership concerns**: Passive giants (Vanguard, BlackRock, State Street) control massive voting blocks.

These are valid concerns but don't undermine the core case for index funds for most investors.
