# Stock Valuation

## Why Valuation Matters
Valuation helps determine whether a stock is cheap, fairly priced, or expensive relative to its fundamentals. Buying good companies at fair or low prices improves long-term returns.

## Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
P/E = Stock Price / Earnings Per Share (EPS)

- S&P 500 historical average: ~15-20x
- P/E > 25: Possibly overvalued (or high growth expected)
- P/E < 15: Possibly undervalued (or declining business)
- Negative P/E: Company is losing money

**Forward P/E**: Uses estimated future earnings. More forward-looking but relies on analyst forecasts.

## Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio
P/B = Stock Price / Book Value Per Share

Book value = total assets minus total liabilities. P/B < 1 may indicate undervaluation. Common in banking sector analysis.

## Price-to-Sales (P/S) Ratio
P/S = Market Cap / Annual Revenue

Useful for companies with no earnings yet (early-stage or unprofitable). Lower is generally better.

## PEG Ratio
PEG = P/E Ratio / Earnings Growth Rate

Adjusts P/E for growth. A PEG of 1.0 is considered fairly valued. PEG < 1 may indicate undervaluation relative to growth.

## Dividend Discount Model (DDM)
Values a stock as the present value of all future dividends.
Value = D1 / (r - g)
- D1 = next year's expected dividend
- r = required rate of return
- g = dividend growth rate

Best for stable, dividend-paying companies.

## Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)
Projects future free cash flows and discounts them to present value. Most thorough but highly sensitive to assumptions. Small changes in growth rate or discount rate dramatically change results.

## EV/EBITDA
Enterprise Value / EBITDA. Popular for comparing companies with different capital structures or tax situations. Useful in M&A analysis.

## Relative Valuation
Compare a company's multiples to:
- Its own historical averages
- Industry peers
- Broader market

A stock trading at 10x P/E in a sector averaging 20x may be cheap — or a value trap.

## Growth vs. Value Investing
- **Value investing**: Buy stocks trading below intrinsic value. Associated with Warren Buffett and Benjamin Graham.
- **Growth investing**: Buy companies growing earnings rapidly, even at high multiples. Associated with tech stocks.
- **GARP (Growth at a Reasonable Price)**: Blend of both approaches.
