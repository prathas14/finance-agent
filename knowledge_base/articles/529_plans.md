# 529 Plans: Education Savings

## What Is a 529 Plan?
A 529 plan is a tax-advantaged savings account designed for education expenses. Named after Section 529 of the IRS tax code. Offered by states, managed by investment companies.

## Tax Benefits
- **Contributions**: No federal tax deduction. Many states offer a state income tax deduction or credit (typically for in-state plans).
- **Growth**: Tax-free.
- **Withdrawals**: Tax-free when used for qualified education expenses.

## Qualified Education Expenses
- College tuition, fees, books, supplies, room and board.
- K-12 tuition: Up to $10,000/year per student.
- Apprenticeship programs registered with the Department of Labor.
- Student loan repayment: Up to $10,000 lifetime per beneficiary.
- Computer and internet access (if required by school).

## Contribution Limits
No annual contribution limit (only gift tax considerations). Total account balance limits set by each state (typically $300,000–$500,000+).

**Gift tax**: Contributions are treated as gifts. Annual gift tax exclusion is $18,000/person (2024). 5-year superfunding: contribute up to $90,000 at once (5 × $18,000), treated as spread over 5 years for gift tax purposes.

## Who Can Open One?
Any US citizen or resident. You can open a plan in any state, not just your home state. Parent, grandparent, other family member, or even the student can be the account owner.

## Choosing a 529 Plan
Consider:
1. **Your state's tax deduction** — if significant, use your home state's plan.
2. **Investment options and expense ratios** — look for low-cost index fund options.
3. **Plan ratings** — Morningstar rates 529 plans annually.

Top-rated plans: Utah my529, New York 529 Direct, Nevada Vanguard 529.

## Changing the Beneficiary
You can change the beneficiary to another family member at any time. Useful if one child doesn't need the funds but a sibling does.

## Non-Qualified Withdrawals
If funds are not used for qualified expenses:
- Earnings subject to income tax + 10% penalty.
- Contributions are never penalized (you put in after-tax money).

## 529 to Roth IRA Rollover (SECURE 2.0 Act, 2024)
New rule: After 15 years, leftover 529 funds can be rolled into a Roth IRA for the beneficiary:
- Lifetime maximum: $35,000
- Subject to annual Roth IRA contribution limits
- Account must be at least 15 years old
- Great escape valve for overfunded 529s

## Coverdell ESA
Alternative education savings account:
- Contribution limit: $2,000/year
- Income limits apply (phases out above $95,000 single / $190,000 married)
- Can be used for K-12 expenses broadly (not just tuition)
- Must be used by age 30
- Less popular than 529 due to lower limits and income restrictions
