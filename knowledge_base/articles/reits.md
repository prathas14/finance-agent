# REITs: Real Estate Investment Trusts

## What Is a REIT?
A Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) is a company that owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate. REITs allow ordinary investors to invest in large-scale real estate portfolios — office buildings, shopping malls, apartments, hospitals, data centers — without buying property directly.

## How REITs Work
By law, REITs must:
- Invest at least 75% of total assets in real estate.
- Earn at least 75% of gross income from real estate (rent, mortgage interest, property sales).
- Distribute at least **90% of taxable income** to shareholders as dividends each year.
- Have at least 100 shareholders, with no five shareholders owning more than 50%.

The 90% distribution requirement means REITs pay high dividends but retain little for reinvestment.

## REIT Types

### Equity REITs
Own and operate properties. Income comes from rents. Most REITs are equity REITs.
- **Residential**: Apartments (Equity Residential, AvalonBay)
- **Office**: Office buildings (Boston Properties)
- **Retail**: Malls, shopping centers (Simon Property Group)
- **Industrial**: Warehouses, logistics (Prologis)
- **Healthcare**: Hospitals, senior housing (Welltower, Healthpeak)
- **Data Centers**: Server farms (Equinix, Digital Realty)
- **Cell Towers**: Telecom infrastructure (American Tower, Crown Castle)

### Mortgage REITs (mREITs)
Invest in mortgages and mortgage-backed securities rather than physical properties. Income from interest. Highly sensitive to interest rate changes. Higher yield but more volatile.

### Hybrid REITs
Combination of equity and mortgage REITs.

## Why Invest in REITs?
- **High dividend yield**: Typically 3–5%+ (higher than stocks generally).
- **Inflation hedge**: Rents and property values tend to rise with inflation.
- **Diversification**: Low correlation to stocks historically.
- **Liquidity**: Publicly traded REITs sell like stocks (vs. illiquid direct real estate).
- **No landlord hassles**: Professional management.

## REIT ETFs
- **VNQ** (Vanguard Real Estate ETF): Broad US REIT exposure, 0.12% expense ratio.
- **SCHH** (Schwab US REIT ETF): Low-cost alternative.
- **REET**: Global REIT ETF.
- **IYR** (iShares US Real Estate): Slightly broader than VNQ.

## Taxation of REIT Dividends
REIT dividends are mostly classified as ordinary income (not qualified dividends), so they're taxed at your marginal rate. The 20% pass-through deduction (Section 199A) may apply, reducing effective rate.

**Best held in tax-advantaged accounts** (IRA, 401k) to avoid the ordinary income tax hit.

## REITs vs. Direct Real Estate
| Feature | REITs | Direct Real Estate |
|---------|-------|-------------------|
| Liquidity | High (daily) | Low (months to sell) |
| Minimum investment | $1 (via ETF) | $20,000–$200,000+ down payment |
| Leverage | Low (institutional) | High (mortgage) |
| Management | Professional | You (or property manager) |
| Tax benefits | Limited | Depreciation, 1031 exchange |
| Concentration | Diversified | Single property risk |
