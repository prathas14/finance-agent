# Expense Ratios and Investment Costs

## Why Costs Matter So Much
Investment costs compound just like returns — but against you. A 1% difference in annual fees over 30 years reduces your final portfolio value by roughly 25%.

Example: $100,000 invested for 30 years at 7% gross return:
- 0.05% expense ratio → $757,000
- 1.00% expense ratio → $574,000
- 2.00% expense ratio → $432,000

That 2% annual fee costs you $325,000 in lost compounding.

## Expense Ratio
Annual fee charged by a fund to cover operating costs (management, administration, legal, marketing). Expressed as a percentage of assets. Deducted daily — you never see a bill, it's reflected in NAV.

### Typical Expense Ratios
| Fund Type | Typical Range |
|-----------|--------------|
| Vanguard/Schwab/Fidelity index ETF | 0.03–0.10% |
| S&P 500 index fund | 0.03–0.15% |
| Active equity fund | 0.50–1.50% |
| Target-date fund | 0.10–0.75% |
| Hedge fund | 2.00% + 20% of profits |

## Other Costs to Know

### Trading Commissions
Most major brokers (Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard, Robinhood) now offer $0 commission on stock and ETF trades. Some still charge for mutual fund transactions.

### Bid-Ask Spread
The difference between the buy price and sell price of an ETF or stock. On liquid ETFs this is pennies. On illiquid ones it can be significant.

### Sales Loads (Mutual Funds)
- **Front-end load**: Commission paid when you buy (e.g. 5.75% of investment goes to advisor).
- **Back-end load (CDSC)**: Commission paid when you sell, typically declining over time.
- **No-load funds**: No sales commission. Preferred by self-directed investors.

### 12b-1 Fees
Annual marketing and distribution fee (up to 0.25% for "no-load" funds, up to 1% for others). Included in the expense ratio but worth noting.

### Tax Drag
In taxable accounts, frequent trading inside a fund creates taxable events for shareholders. Index funds have lower turnover and are more tax-efficient.

## Total Cost of Ownership
True cost = expense ratio + trading costs + tax drag + opportunity cost of sales loads.

A 0.10% index ETF held in a tax-advantaged account with $0 commissions has near-zero total cost.
