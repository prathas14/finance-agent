# Bonds and Fixed Income

## What Is a Bond?
A bond is a loan you make to a government or corporation. In return, they pay you interest (the coupon) and return the principal at maturity.

## Key Terms
- **Face Value / Par Value**: The amount repaid at maturity (usually $1,000).
- **Coupon Rate**: Annual interest rate paid on the face value.
- **Maturity**: When the principal is returned.
- **Yield**: The effective return, accounting for current price vs. face value.

## Bond Types
- **US Treasury Bonds**: Backed by the US government. Safest. Lower yield.
- **Municipal Bonds (Munis)**: Issued by states/cities. Often tax-exempt.
- **Corporate Bonds**: Higher yield, higher risk than Treasuries.
- **I-Bonds**: Inflation-protected savings bonds. Great for emergency funds.

## Interest Rate Risk
Bond prices move *inversely* to interest rates. When rates rise, existing bond prices fall. Longer-duration bonds are more sensitive.

## Role in a Portfolio
Bonds reduce portfolio volatility. A classic allocation is:
- Young investor: 90% stocks / 10% bonds
- Near retirement: 60% stocks / 40% bonds
- In retirement: 40% stocks / 60% bonds
