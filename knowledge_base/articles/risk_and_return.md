# Risk and Return

## The Fundamental Trade-off
In investing, risk and return are inseparable. Higher potential returns require accepting higher risk. There is no free lunch — any investment promising high returns with no risk is a red flag.

## Types of Investment Risk
- **Market risk (systematic)**: Risk affecting the entire market — recessions, interest rate changes, geopolitical events. Cannot be diversified away.
- **Company risk (unsystematic)**: Risk specific to one company or industry — bad earnings, CEO scandal, product recall. Can be reduced through diversification.
- **Inflation risk**: Risk that returns won't keep pace with inflation.
- **Liquidity risk**: Risk of not being able to sell an investment quickly at a fair price.
- **Concentration risk**: Risk from having too much in one stock, sector, or asset class.
- **Sequence of returns risk**: Risk of poor returns early in retirement depleting your portfolio before recovery.

## Measuring Risk
- **Standard deviation**: Measures how much returns vary from the average. A fund with 10% average return and 15% std dev will typically return between -5% and 25% in a given year.
- **Beta**: Measures a stock's volatility relative to the market. Beta > 1 = more volatile than market. Beta < 1 = less volatile.
- **Sharpe Ratio**: (Return - Risk-free rate) / Standard deviation. Measures return per unit of risk. Higher is better.
- **Max drawdown**: The largest peak-to-trough decline. Useful for understanding worst-case scenarios.

## Risk Tolerance vs. Risk Capacity
- **Risk tolerance**: How much volatility you can emotionally handle without panic-selling.
- **Risk capacity**: How much risk your financial situation can objectively absorb.

A 30-year-old with stable income and no near-term cash needs has high capacity even if low tolerance.

## Managing Risk
1. **Diversify** across asset classes, geographies, and sectors.
2. **Match time horizon** — longer horizons tolerate more risk.
3. **Keep an emergency fund** so you never sell investments at the wrong time.
4. **Rebalance** regularly to prevent unintended risk drift.
5. **Understand what you own** — don't invest in things you can't explain.
