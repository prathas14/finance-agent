# Financial Planning Through Life Stages

## Why Life Stage Matters
Financial priorities shift dramatically as you age. A 22-year-old's biggest lever is time for compounding. A 55-year-old's biggest concern is preserving wealth and planning retirement income. Advice that fits one stage can be wrong for another.

## Stage 1: Early Career (Ages 22–35)

### Financial Priorities
1. Build emergency fund (3 months of expenses).
2. Eliminate high-interest debt (credit cards first).
3. Get the full employer 401(k) match — free money.
4. Open and max a Roth IRA ($7,000/year).
5. Start investing — time is your most valuable asset.

### Why Roth > Traditional at This Stage
You're likely in a lower tax bracket now than you will be at peak career. Pay taxes now at a low rate; withdraw tax-free in retirement at a higher rate.

### Common Mistakes
- Delaying investing until debt is paid off (except high-interest debt — that should be paid first).
- Not investing in a 401(k) due to complexity — just use the target-date fund.
- Lifestyle inflation after salary increases — save raises before you get used to spending them.

## Stage 2: Mid Career and Family Formation (Ages 35–50)

### Financial Priorities
1. Maximize retirement contributions (401k + IRA).
2. Save for children's education (529 plan).
3. Buy adequate life insurance (term) if you have dependents.
4. Buy/build a home equity.
5. Accelerate wealth building — peak earning years.

### Wealth Accumulation
This is typically the highest earning period. Savings rate has enormous impact. Every 1% increase in savings rate meaningfully changes retirement outcomes.

### Common Mistakes
- Over-allocating to children's college at expense of retirement. "You can borrow for school; you cannot borrow for retirement."
- Under-insured during maximum dependency period.
- Keeping too conservative a portfolio — 30 years of growth remaining.

## Stage 3: Pre-Retirement (Ages 50–65)

### Financial Priorities
1. Maximize catch-up contributions (extra $7,500 to 401k, $1,000 to IRA).
2. Start modeling Social Security claiming strategy.
3. Consider Roth conversions during lower-income years.
4. Gradually reduce equity risk (not all at once).
5. Pay off mortgage before retirement if possible.

### The Sequence of Returns Problem
A 30% crash in year 1 of retirement is far more damaging than the same crash in year 10. As retirement approaches, gradually shift to more stable allocations.

### Healthcare Bridge
Retire before 65 (Medicare eligibility)? You need a plan for health insurance — ACA marketplace, COBRA, spouse's plan, or part-time work with benefits.

## Stage 4: Retirement (Ages 65+)

### Financial Priorities
1. Execute tax-efficient withdrawal strategy.
2. Optimize Social Security timing.
3. Take required minimum distributions (RMDs) from pre-tax accounts.
4. Manage healthcare costs — largest retirement expense.
5. Update estate plan — beneficiary designations, healthcare directives.

### Shifting Goals
From accumulation to preservation and income. Primary risk: outliving your money and healthcare costs, not market volatility.

### Inflation Remains a Risk
A 3% inflation rate doubles prices over 24 years. A 65-year-old may live to 90. Maintaining some equity exposure is necessary to keep pace with inflation over a 25-year retirement.
