# Growth vs. Value Investing

## The Two Philosophies

**Value investing**: Buy stocks that appear underpriced relative to their intrinsic value. Look for low P/E, P/B, or P/S ratios. The idea: the market has temporarily mispriced the stock and it will eventually correct.

**Growth investing**: Buy stocks of companies growing revenues and earnings rapidly, even if current valuation is high. The idea: future earnings will justify today's price.

## Value Investing Principles
Pioneered by Benjamin Graham and popularized by Warren Buffett.

Key tenets:
- Margin of safety: Buy well below estimated intrinsic value to protect against errors.
- Mr. Market: The market is irrational short-term. Use its mood swings to your advantage.
- Circle of competence: Only invest in businesses you understand.
- Long holding periods: "Our favourite holding period is forever." — Buffett

Typical value stocks: banks, utilities, consumer staples, industrial companies.

## Growth Investing Principles
Associated with Philip Fisher, Peter Lynch, and modern tech investors.

Key tenets:
- Invest in companies with durable competitive advantages (moats).
- Look for expanding total addressable market (TAM).
- Revenue growth matters more than current profitability for early-stage companies.
- Management quality is critical.

Typical growth stocks: technology, healthcare innovation, consumer disruptors.

## Performance Across Market Cycles
- Value tends to outperform during rising interest rate environments and economic recoveries.
- Growth tends to outperform during low interest rate periods and economic expansions.
- Neither consistently beats the other over all periods.

## GARP: Growth at a Reasonable Price
A middle-ground approach popularised by Peter Lynch. Buy growth companies but only at sensible valuations (low PEG ratio). Avoids overpaying for growth.

## Factor Investing
Both value and growth are "factors" — systematic characteristics that have historically delivered excess returns. Other factors include:
- **Momentum**: Stocks rising tend to keep rising short-term.
- **Quality**: High return on equity, low debt, stable earnings.
- **Size**: Small-cap stocks have historically outperformed large-cap long-term.

## Which Is Better?
Academic research shows value has outperformed over the very long run, but growth dominated in the 2010s. Most long-term investors do best by blending both through diversified index funds.
