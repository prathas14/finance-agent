# Capital Gains Tax

## What Is a Capital Gain?
A capital gain occurs when you sell an asset for more than you paid for it. The gain is the difference between the sale price (proceeds) and your cost basis.

Capital gain = Sale price - Cost basis

## Short-Term vs. Long-Term Capital Gains

### Short-Term Capital Gains
Assets held **1 year or less**. Taxed as **ordinary income** at your marginal rate (10%–37%).

### Long-Term Capital Gains
Assets held **more than 1 year**. Taxed at preferential rates (0%, 15%, or 20%) — significantly lower than ordinary income rates.

### 2024 Long-Term Capital Gains Rates (Single Filer)
| Taxable Income | Rate |
|---------------|------|
| $0 – $47,025 | 0% |
| $47,026 – $518,900 | 15% |
| Over $518,900 | 20% |

**Key insight**: A middle-income investor in the 22% ordinary income bracket pays only 15% on long-term gains. This is a major incentive to hold investments for over a year.

## Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT)
An additional 3.8% tax on investment income (including capital gains) for high earners:
- Single: income over $200,000
- Married filing jointly: income over $250,000

This makes the effective top rate on long-term gains 23.8%.

## Cost Basis Methods
How you calculate cost basis affects your taxable gain:
- **FIFO (First In, First Out)**: Default for stocks. Oldest shares sold first.
- **Specific identification**: Choose which shares to sell. Best for tax optimization.
- **Average cost**: Average of all purchase prices. Default for mutual funds.

Tax tip: When selling appreciated shares, identify your highest-cost-basis shares to minimize gain.

## Tax-Loss Harvesting
Selling investments at a loss to offset capital gains and reduce taxes.

Rules:
- Capital losses first offset same-type gains (short vs. long).
- Excess losses can offset up to $3,000 of ordinary income per year.
- Additional losses carry forward to future years indefinitely.

**Wash-sale rule**: You cannot claim a loss if you buy the "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after the sale. You can sell VTI and immediately buy ITOT (different funds, similar index — but technically not identical).

## Capital Gains on Your Home
- Exclude up to $250,000 of gain ($500,000 married) on primary residence sale.
- Must have lived in the home 2 of the past 5 years.
- Gains above the exclusion are taxed as long-term capital gains (if owned 1+ year).

## Gifting Appreciated Stock
Gifting appreciated stock to charity or family is a powerful tax strategy:
- To charity: Deduct full market value, pay no capital gains tax.
- To lower-income family member: They sell it at their (potentially 0%) capital gains rate.

## Step-Up in Basis at Death
When you inherit appreciated assets, your cost basis is "stepped up" to the fair market value at the date of death. This eliminates the capital gains tax on the appreciation during the deceased's lifetime — one of the most significant tax benefits in the US tax code.
