# Tax-Advantaged Accounts: Complete Overview

## Why Tax-Advantaged Accounts Are Critical
Taxes are the largest single drag on investment returns. A 22% tax bracket investor paying taxes annually on 7% returns effectively earns ~5.5%. The same investor using tax-advantaged accounts effectively earns the full 7% (or more, with deductions).

Over 30 years, the difference is enormous.

## The Account Priority Hierarchy
For most workers (general guidance):
1. **401(k) to employer match** — free money, always first.
2. **HSA to max** — triple tax advantage (best account in tax code).
3. **Roth IRA to max** — tax-free growth, flexible.
4. **Back to 401(k) to max** — tax-deferred growth.
5. **529 if applicable** — state deduction + tax-free growth for education.
6. **Taxable brokerage** — no tax benefit but no restrictions.

## Traditional vs. Roth: The Decision Framework
The right choice depends on your expected tax rate now vs. in retirement.

**Prefer Traditional (pre-tax) when**:
- In peak earning years (high bracket now).
- Expect lower income in retirement.
- Need the immediate tax deduction.

**Prefer Roth when**:
- Early career (low bracket now, higher expected in future).
- Expect higher taxes in retirement.
- Want tax diversification and no RMDs.
- Under income limits for Roth IRA contribution.

**When in doubt**: Hold both for tax diversification. Having both pre-tax and Roth accounts gives flexibility in retirement to manage taxable income.

## Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)
Pre-tax accounts (Traditional IRA, 401k) require minimum withdrawals starting at age 73.
Roth IRAs have NO RMDs during owner's lifetime.
Roth 401(k)s are now also exempt from RMDs (SECURE 2.0, 2024).

## Contribution Limits Are Per Person, Not Per Account
You can have both a Traditional IRA and a Roth IRA, but the total contributions across both cannot exceed $7,000/$8,000.

You can have both a 401(k) and an IRA — these have separate limits.

You can have both a 401(k) and a SEP-IRA (if self-employed income) — complex but legal.

## Tax Location: Where to Hold What
Maximise tax efficiency by placing the right assets in the right accounts:

**Tax-advantaged (IRA/401k)**:
- Bonds (interest income taxed as ordinary income — defer it)
- REITs (high ordinary income dividends)
- High-yield / actively traded funds

**Taxable brokerage**:
- Total market / S&P 500 index funds (tax-efficient, low turnover)
- Municipal bonds (already tax-free)
- Tax-managed funds
