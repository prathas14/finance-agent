# Tax-Loss Harvesting

## What Is Tax-Loss Harvesting?
Tax-loss harvesting (TLH) is the practice of selling investments that have declined in value to realize a loss, which can offset capital gains and reduce your tax bill — while maintaining approximately the same market exposure by immediately buying a similar (but not identical) investment.

## How It Works: Step by Step
1. You own Fund A (e.g., SPY) and it's down $5,000 from your cost basis.
2. You sell SPY, realizing a $5,000 capital loss.
3. You immediately buy a similar but not identical fund (e.g., IVV or VOO — also S&P 500, different issuer).
4. You're still invested in the market. Your tax loss is captured.

## Tax Benefit
Capital losses offset:
1. Capital gains of the same type first (short-term losses → short-term gains; long-term losses → long-term gains).
2. Gains of the opposite type (excess short-term losses can offset long-term gains).
3. Up to $3,000 of ordinary income per year.
4. Remaining losses carry forward indefinitely to future years.

Example: You have $15,000 in capital gains this year and $20,000 in harvested losses. The $20,000 loss wipes out $15,000 of gains, offsets $3,000 of ordinary income, and $2,000 carries to next year.

## The Wash-Sale Rule
The IRS forbids claiming a loss if you buy a "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after the sale (the 30-day window each direction = 61 days total).

### What counts as "substantially identical"?
- Same ETF: Selling SPY and buying SPY the next day → wash sale.
- Same fund class: Selling Vanguard Admiral and buying Investor shares → wash sale.
- Generally NOT wash sales: SPY → IVV (same index, different issuer), VTI → ITOT.

**Important**: Wash-sale rules apply across all accounts you own (and your spouse's accounts). Harvesting a loss in a taxable account and buying the same fund in your IRA could trigger the wash-sale rule.

## Automated Tax-Loss Harvesting
Robo-advisors like Betterment and Wealthfront offer daily automated TLH. They monitor portfolios for loss opportunities and execute trades automatically.

## When TLH Is Most Valuable
- High tax bracket (long-term gains rate of 15-20%).
- Large capital gains to offset from selling a business, property, or concentrated stock.
- Volatile markets create more loss opportunities.
- Early in the year — harvested losses can offset gains throughout the year.

## Limitations
- Defers taxes, doesn't eliminate them. When you sell the replacement fund, its cost basis is lower (the harvested loss), creating a larger future gain.
- Only valuable in taxable accounts (not IRAs or 401(k)s — no taxes there anyway).
- Transaction costs must be weighed against tax savings.
- Complexity — easy to make wash-sale mistakes across multiple accounts.
