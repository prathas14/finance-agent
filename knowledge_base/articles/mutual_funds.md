# Mutual Funds

## What Is a Mutual Fund?
A mutual fund pools money from many investors to buy a diversified portfolio of securities. Unlike ETFs, mutual fund shares are bought and sold at end-of-day NAV (net asset value), not throughout the day.

## How Mutual Funds Work
1. Investors buy shares directly from the fund company (e.g. Vanguard, Fidelity, Schwab).
2. The fund's portfolio manager invests pooled capital according to the fund's mandate.
3. At market close, NAV is calculated: (Total assets - liabilities) / shares outstanding.
4. All purchases and redemptions that day execute at that closing NAV.

## Types of Mutual Funds

### By Strategy
- **Active funds**: Manager selects securities to beat a benchmark. Higher fees.
- **Index funds**: Passively track an index. Low fees. Most outperform active funds long-term.
- **Target-date funds**: Automatically shift from stocks to bonds as a target retirement year approaches. Simple one-fund solution.

### By Asset Class
- **Equity funds**: Invest in stocks (domestic, international, sector).
- **Bond funds**: Invest in fixed-income securities.
- **Balanced/hybrid funds**: Mix of stocks and bonds.
- **Money market funds**: Very short-term, high-quality debt. Very low risk. Used as cash equivalent.

## Key Metrics
- **NAV**: Net asset value per share.
- **Expense ratio**: Annual cost as a % of assets. Index funds: 0.01–0.20%. Active: 0.50–1.5%+.
- **Load**: Sales commission. Front-end load (charged at purchase), back-end load (charged at sale). No-load funds have no commission.
- **Turnover rate**: How frequently the fund buys/sells. High turnover → higher taxes in taxable accounts.
- **Minimum investment**: Many require $1,000–$3,000 to start.

## Mutual Funds vs. ETFs
| Feature | Mutual Fund | ETF |
|---------|------------|-----|
| Trading | End of day NAV | Real-time on exchange |
| Minimum investment | Often $1,000+ | Price of 1 share (or $1 with fractional) |
| Expense ratio | Varies | Generally lower |
| Tax efficiency | Lower (capital gains distributions) | Higher |
| Auto-invest | Easy (dollar amount) | Requires fractional shares |

## The Active vs. Passive Debate
Research consistently shows that:
- ~80-90% of active funds underperform their benchmark over 10-20 years.
- After fees, it's extremely difficult to consistently beat the market.
- Jack Bogle (founder of Vanguard) championed index funds based on this evidence.

**The Vanguard S&P 500 Index Fund (VFIAX)** is the largest mutual fund in the world — evidence of the shift to passive investing.

## Target-Date Funds
A complete portfolio in one fund. Choose your retirement year and the fund automatically adjusts:
- Far from retirement: 90% stocks / 10% bonds
- Near retirement: 50% stocks / 50% bonds
- At/in retirement: 30% stocks / 70% bonds

Popular options: Vanguard Target Retirement series, Fidelity Freedom funds, Schwab Target Date series.
