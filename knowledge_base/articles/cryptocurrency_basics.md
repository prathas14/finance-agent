# Cryptocurrency Basics

## What Is Cryptocurrency?
Cryptocurrency is a digital currency secured by cryptography and typically operating on a decentralised blockchain network — no central bank or government controls it. Bitcoin was the first, created in 2009 by the pseudonymous Satoshi Nakamoto.

## How Blockchain Works
A blockchain is a distributed ledger: a chain of transaction records shared across thousands of computers worldwide. Once recorded, data cannot be altered without changing all subsequent blocks and gaining consensus from the network.

Key properties:
- **Decentralised**: No single point of control or failure.
- **Transparent**: All transactions publicly visible.
- **Immutable**: Records cannot be changed.
- **Trustless**: No need for intermediaries (banks).

## Major Cryptocurrencies

### Bitcoin (BTC)
- First and largest cryptocurrency by market cap.
- Fixed supply: 21 million coins maximum.
- "Digital gold" narrative — store of value, not primarily for transactions.
- Proof-of-work consensus (mining — high energy use).

### Ethereum (ETH)
- Smart contract platform — programmable blockchain.
- Powers DeFi (decentralised finance), NFTs, and thousands of apps.
- Proof-of-stake consensus since 2022 (The Merge) — ~99% less energy than Bitcoin.

### Stablecoins
Pegged to fiat currency (usually $1 USD). Used for transactions, DeFi, avoiding volatility.
- USDC (Circle): Fully backed by cash and Treasuries.
- USDT (Tether): Largest by volume, some regulatory scrutiny.
- DAI: Algorithmic, decentralised.

## Crypto Risks
- **Extreme volatility**: Bitcoin has fallen 80-90% multiple times and recovered. Smaller coins are far more volatile.
- **Regulatory risk**: Government bans or regulations can devastate prices.
- **Security risk**: Exchanges get hacked. Self-custody has its own risks (losing private keys).
- **Fraud and scams**: Crypto is rife with rug pulls, Ponzi schemes, and fraud.
- **No intrinsic cash flows**: Unlike stocks (earnings) or bonds (coupons), crypto has no underlying cash flows to value.
- **Technological risk**: Better technology could make existing coins obsolete.
- **Tax complexity**: Every trade is a taxable event in the US.

## Crypto Taxation (US)
- **Buying crypto**: Not taxable.
- **Selling crypto for USD**: Capital gains/loss.
- **Trading crypto-to-crypto**: Taxable event (gain/loss calculated).
- **Receiving crypto as income**: Ordinary income at fair market value.
- **Mining rewards**: Ordinary income.
Long-term rates apply if held >1 year. Track every transaction — required for tax filing.

## Portfolio Allocation
Given extreme volatility and speculative nature, most financial advisors suggest:
- 0% for conservative investors.
- 1-5% maximum for investors comfortable with potential total loss.
- Treat as speculative, high-risk position — not core portfolio.

## Crypto in Tax-Advantaged Accounts
- **Bitcoin ETFs** (approved by SEC in January 2024): Can be held in regular brokerage, IRA, 401k.
- **Crypto IRAs**: Exist but often have high fees and limited options.
- **Direct crypto in 401k**: Very rare — Fidelity offers it, most don't.

## Self-Custody
Storing crypto in your own wallet (hardware wallet like Ledger/Trezor) rather than on an exchange. "Not your keys, not your coins" — exchange collapses (FTX, Celsius, Mt. Gox) have wiped out billions.
