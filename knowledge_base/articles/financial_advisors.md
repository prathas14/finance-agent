# Financial Advisors

## Types of Financial Advisors

### Fee-Only Fiduciary Advisors
Paid directly by clients (flat fee, hourly, or AUM percentage). No commissions. Legally required to act in your best interest.

This is the gold standard. Search at NAPFA.org (National Association of Personal Financial Advisors) or garrettplanningnetwork.com.

### Fee-Based Advisors
Charge fees AND earn commissions on products they sell. Potential conflicts of interest.

### Commission-Only Advisors (Brokers)
Earn commissions selling financial products. Held to the lower "suitability" standard, not the fiduciary standard. They recommend products that are "suitable," not necessarily the best for you.

## Fiduciary vs. Suitability Standard

**Fiduciary standard**: Must recommend the option that is in YOUR best interest, even if it means less compensation for them.

**Suitability standard**: Must only recommend products that are "suitable" for you — a much lower bar. A 1.5% expense ratio actively managed fund is "suitable" even if a 0.05% index fund would serve you better.

Always ask: "Are you a fiduciary? In writing?"

## Fee Structures

### AUM (Assets Under Management)
1% of portfolio value per year is typical. On $500,000: $5,000/year. Scales with portfolio — aligns incentives to grow your assets, but incentivises against paying off mortgage or other non-managed assets.

### Hourly
$200–$400/hour. Good for one-time advice or specific questions. No ongoing relationship necessary.

### Flat Annual Retainer
$2,000–$10,000/year. Comprehensive planning for a fixed cost. Not tied to assets.

### Per Plan
One-time comprehensive financial plan: $1,500–$5,000.

## When Do You Need a Financial Advisor?
Not everyone does. Simple financial situations (steady income, max retirement accounts, three-fund portfolio) don't require an advisor.

Consider an advisor when:
- You've received a large inheritance or windfall.
- Selling a business.
- Complex tax situation (stock options, business ownership, multiple states).
- Divorce — financial implications are complex.
- Approaching retirement — withdrawal strategy optimisation.
- You consistently make emotional investment decisions despite best intentions.
- Estate planning is complex.

## Red Flags
- Promises specific investment returns.
- Recommends annuities, whole life insurance, or complex products without clear explanation of fees.
- Won't confirm fiduciary status in writing.
- Charges significant commissions.
- Earns referral fees or "kickbacks" from recommended products.
- Doesn't explain the total cost of their recommendations.

## Finding an Advisor
- **NAPFA.org**: Fee-only fiduciary advisors.
- **XY Planning Network**: Fee-only advisors focused on Gen X/Y.
- **Garrett Planning Network**: Hourly/project-based fee-only advisors.
- **CFP Board**: Find Certified Financial Planners (cfp.net).

The CFP (Certified Financial Planner) designation requires education, exam, experience, and ethics requirements — a meaningful credential.
