# Emergency Fund

## What Is an Emergency Fund?
An emergency fund is money set aside specifically for unexpected, necessary expenses — job loss, medical emergency, car or home repair. It is the foundation of any financial plan.

## Why It Is Non-Negotiable
Without an emergency fund:
- Unexpected expenses go on credit cards at 20%+ APR.
- You're forced to sell investments at the worst time.
- Financial stress affects health, relationships, and career.
- One bad event cascades into multiple problems.

## How Much to Save
### Standard Guidance: 3–6 Months of Expenses
Calculate your essential monthly expenses:
- Rent/mortgage
- Utilities
- Food
- Transportation
- Minimum debt payments
- Insurance premiums

Multiply by 3–6 to get your target.

### When to Use Which End of the Range
| Situation | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| Dual income household, stable jobs | 3 months |
| Single income, stable job | 4-5 months |
| Variable income (freelancer, commission) | 6+ months |
| Single income, job insecurity | 6+ months |
| Industry with volatile employment | 9-12 months |

## Where to Keep It
The emergency fund has one job: be there when you need it. Prioritize safety and liquidity over return.

### Best Options
- **High-yield savings account (HYSA)**: Online banks (Marcus, Ally, Discover, Marcus) offer 4-5% APY. FDIC insured. Instant transfer to checking. Best default choice.
- **Money market account**: Similar rates, FDIC/NCUA insured. Some offer check-writing.
- **Treasury bills (short-term)**: Slightly higher yield, state tax exempt. Minor liquidity delay (T+1 settlement).

### Do NOT Use These for Emergency Fund
- **Stocks or stock ETFs**: Down 30% when you need money most (recessions cause job losses too).
- **CDs**: Early withdrawal penalties.
- **I-Bonds**: 1-year lockup, 3-month penalty for early redemption.
- **Home equity**: Takes weeks to access.

## Building Your Emergency Fund
### Step 1: $1,000 Mini Fund
Get the first $1,000 as fast as possible. This handles most common emergencies (car repair, medical bill) without touching credit cards.

### Step 2: Pay Off High-Interest Debt
Once you have the $1,000 buffer, aggressively eliminate high-interest debt.

### Step 3: Full Fund
After clearing high-rate debt, build to your 3–6 month target.

## Replenishing After Use
If you use the fund, replenish it immediately. Redirect what you were investing/saving until it's back to target. You've just proven the value of having it — don't delay rebuilding.

## Common Mistakes
- Keeping emergency fund in a regular savings account earning 0.01% when HYSAs offer 4.5%+.
- Using it for non-emergencies (vacation, new phone).
- Investing it in hopes of higher returns (creates risk exactly when you need certainty).
- Not having one at all — most common and most dangerous.
