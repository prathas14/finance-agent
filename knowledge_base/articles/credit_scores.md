# Credit Scores

## What Is a Credit Score?
A credit score is a numerical representation of your creditworthiness — how likely you are to repay debt. Lenders use it to decide whether to approve loans and at what interest rate.

## FICO Score: The Industry Standard
FICO (Fair Isaac Corporation) scores are used in ~90% of lending decisions. Range: 300–850. Higher is better.

### Score Ranges
| Score | Category | Impact |
|-------|----------|--------|
| 800–850 | Exceptional | Best rates available |
| 740–799 | Very Good | Near-best rates |
| 670–739 | Good | Approved with decent rates |
| 580–669 | Fair | Approved but higher rates |
| Below 580 | Poor | Likely denied or very high rates |

## FICO Score Components
| Factor | Weight | What It Measures |
|--------|--------|-----------------|
| Payment history | 35% | On-time payments (most important) |
| Amounts owed | 30% | Credit utilization ratio |
| Length of credit history | 15% | Age of oldest, newest, and average account |
| New credit | 10% | Recent hard inquiries and new accounts |
| Credit mix | 10% | Variety of credit types |

## Credit Utilization
The most actionable factor after payment history. Utilization = Balance / Credit limit.

- Keep below 30% on each card and overall.
- Ideally below 10% for excellent scores.
- $500 balance on a $1,000 limit = 50% utilization → hurts score.
- $500 balance on a $5,000 limit = 10% utilization → helps score.

## Building Credit from Scratch
1. **Secured credit card**: Deposit $200–500 as collateral, use for small purchases, pay in full monthly.
2. **Become an authorized user**: Ask a family member with good credit to add you.
3. **Credit-builder loan**: Small loan where funds are held in savings while you make payments.
4. **Student credit card**: Designed for thin files.

## Improving Your Score
- Pay every bill on time, every time — automate minimums.
- Pay down credit card balances (reduce utilization).
- Don't close old accounts — keeps history length and available credit.
- Request credit limit increases (lowers utilization ratio).
- Space out applications for new credit.

## Hard vs. Soft Inquiries
- **Hard inquiry**: Lender checks credit for a loan/card application. Temporarily drops score 2–5 points. Stays on report 2 years.
- **Soft inquiry**: Checking your own score, pre-approval checks. Does NOT affect score.

Multiple mortgage or auto loan inquiries within 14–45 days count as one inquiry (rate shopping protected).

## Credit Reports vs. Credit Scores
- **Credit report**: Detailed record of your credit history. Free annually at AnnualCreditReport.com.
- **Credit score**: Numerical summary calculated from your report.

Check your report annually for errors — 1 in 5 reports contain errors. Dispute inaccuracies with the credit bureaus (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion).

## Credit Freezes
Free to place and lift. Prevents new accounts from being opened in your name. Best identity theft protection. Does not affect your score or existing accounts. Recommended for everyone not actively applying for credit.

## How Credit Affects Your Financial Life
- Mortgage rate difference between 620 and 760 score: ~1.5% higher rate.
- On a $400,000 30-year mortgage, that's ~$130,000 more in interest.
- Affects car insurance rates in most states.
- Landlords check credit for rental applications.
- Some employers check credit for certain positions.
