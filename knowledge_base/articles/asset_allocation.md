# Asset Allocation

## What Is Asset Allocation?
Asset allocation is the process of dividing your investment portfolio among different asset classes — stocks, bonds, cash, real estate, etc. It's the most important investment decision you make, responsible for over 90% of portfolio return variability according to academic research.

## Why Asset Allocation Matters
Different asset classes respond differently to economic conditions. When stocks fall, bonds often rise. This non-correlation reduces overall portfolio volatility without sacrificing as much return.

## Core Asset Classes
- **Equities (stocks)**: Highest long-term return potential. High volatility.
- **Fixed income (bonds)**: Lower return, lower volatility. Provides stability.
- **Cash/equivalents**: Money market, T-bills, CDs. Lowest return, highest safety.
- **Real assets**: Real estate (REITs), commodities, gold. Inflation protection.
- **International**: Developed and emerging market equities/bonds.

## Age-Based Rules of Thumb

### Classic Rule: "100 minus your age"
Percentage in stocks = 100 - your age.
At 30: 70% stocks / 30% bonds.
At 60: 40% stocks / 60% bonds.

### Modern Adjustment: "110 or 120 minus your age"
Because people live longer and need more growth, many advisors now use 110 or 120:
At 30: 80-90% stocks.
At 60: 50-60% stocks.

## Beyond Age: Other Factors
- **Risk tolerance**: Can you sleep at night with 30% drawdowns? Reduce stocks if not.
- **Time horizon**: Longer horizon → more stocks.
- **Income stability**: Stable job → can take more risk.
- **Other assets**: Pension, rental income, inheritance → can reduce portfolio risk.
- **Goals**: Short-term goals need conservative allocations.

## Diversification Within Asset Classes
Don't just diversify across asset classes — diversify within them:
- Stocks: US + international + small cap + large cap + growth + value
- Bonds: Short-term + long-term + government + corporate + TIPS
- Real estate: Domestic + international REITs

## The Three-Fund Portfolio
Simple, effective, proven approach:
1. US total stock market (VTI)
2. International total stock market (VXUS)
3. US total bond market (BND)

Adjust the ratio to match your risk tolerance. This beats most actively managed portfolios.

## Correlation and Diversification
True diversification comes from low or negative correlation between assets. In the 2008 crisis, most stocks fell together — correlation spiked. Gold and Treasuries held up better.

## Rebalancing
Over time, market movements drift your allocation from targets. An 80/20 portfolio after a bull market might become 90/10.

Rebalancing approaches:
- **Calendar rebalancing**: Check annually or quarterly.
- **Threshold rebalancing**: Rebalance when any asset drifts 5%+ from target.
- **Cash flow rebalancing**: Direct new contributions to underweight assets (tax-efficient).
