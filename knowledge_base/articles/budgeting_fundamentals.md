# Budgeting Fundamentals

## Why Budget?
A budget is a plan for your money. Without one, money tends to disappear without purpose. A budget reveals exactly where money goes and creates intentionality around financial priorities.

## Popular Budgeting Methods

### 50/30/20 Rule
Simple framework popularised by Senator Elizabeth Warren:
- **50%** of after-tax income → Needs (housing, food, utilities, transportation, minimum debt payments)
- **30%** → Wants (dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, travel)
- **20%** → Savings and extra debt payments

Adjust ratios to your situation. High cost-of-living areas may require 60%+ for needs.

### Zero-Based Budgeting
Give every dollar a job. Income - All expenses/savings = $0.
More detailed and intentional. Popular with Dave Ramsey's system. Requires more time to maintain.

### Pay Yourself First
Automatically move savings/investment contributions out of your account on payday before you can spend them. Budget with what's left. Simplest way to ensure saving happens.

### Envelope System
Allocate cash to physical envelopes for each spending category. When the envelope is empty, you're done spending in that category. Highly effective for controlling discretionary spending. Digital equivalent: separate accounts or apps like YNAB.

## Tracking Spending
You cannot manage what you don't measure. Track every expense for at least 1 month to see where money actually goes (vs. where you think it goes).

### Tools
- **Spreadsheet**: Free, flexible, requires discipline.
- **YNAB (You Need A Budget)**: Proactive, zero-based. ~$100/year. High user success rates.
- **Mint/Copilot/Monarch Money**: Automatic sync with bank accounts. Passive tracking.
- **Personal Capital/Empower**: Good for net worth and investment tracking alongside budgeting.

## The Income vs. Expenses Gap
Wealth is built by widening the gap between income and expenses. Two levers:
1. **Reduce expenses**: More immediate impact, limited floor.
2. **Increase income**: No ceiling. Side income, raises, career moves.

Both matter. Over-focusing on small expenses (the "latte factor") while ignoring large fixed costs (housing, car) is inefficient.

## Large Fixed Costs Are Most Impactful
The 3 biggest expense categories for most households:
1. **Housing**: 30% or less of gross income recommended. Going over here is hard to recover.
2. **Transportation**: Total car costs (payment, insurance, gas, maintenance) under 15% of income.
3. **Food**: Groceries 5-10% of income; eating out 5% or less.

Optimizing these three has far more impact than cutting Netflix or coffee.

## Sinking Funds
Save monthly for predictable irregular expenses (car insurance, holiday gifts, vacation, home maintenance). Calculate annual cost, divide by 12, save that amount monthly into a separate account.

Without sinking funds, irregular expenses feel like "emergencies" and derail your budget.

## Irregular Income
Freelancers, contractors, and commissioned salespeople: budget based on your lowest-income month in the past year. Any surplus in higher months goes to savings or irregular expenses. Treat income as uncertain; treat expenses as fixed.
