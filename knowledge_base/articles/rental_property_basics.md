# Rental Property Basics

## Why Invest in Rental Property?
Rental real estate offers multiple simultaneous returns:
1. **Cash flow**: Monthly rent minus expenses.
2. **Appreciation**: Property value increases over time.
3. **Loan paydown**: Tenants pay down your mortgage.
4. **Tax benefits**: Depreciation, deductions, 1031 exchanges.
5. **Inflation hedge**: Rents and values tend to rise with inflation.

## Key Metrics

### Gross Rental Yield
Annual gross rent / Property value × 100.
$24,000 rent / $400,000 property = 6% gross yield.

### Net Rental Yield
(Annual rent - Annual expenses) / Property value × 100.
More meaningful — accounts for real costs.

### Cap Rate (Capitalization Rate)
Net Operating Income (NOI) / Property value.
NOI = Gross rent - Operating expenses (not including mortgage).
Used to compare properties regardless of financing.

### Cash-on-Cash Return
Annual pre-tax cash flow / Total cash invested.
Measures actual cash return on your down payment. Most relevant for leveraged investors.

### 1% Rule
Monthly rent should be at least 1% of purchase price.
$200,000 property → at least $2,000/month rent.
A rule of thumb, not a guarantee of profitability.

### 50% Rule
Estimate operating expenses at ~50% of gross rent. The other 50% covers mortgage and profit.
Rough screen — actual expenses vary widely.

## Operating Expenses
- Property taxes
- Insurance
- Property management (8–12% of rent if using a manager)
- Maintenance and repairs (budget 1% of property value/year)
- Vacancy (assume 5–8%)
- HOA fees (if applicable)
- Utilities (if landlord-paid)
- Accounting/legal

## Financing
Most investors use leverage (mortgage) to amplify returns. Typical rental property loan:
- 20–25% down payment
- Higher rate than primary residence mortgage
- No PMI required (LTV 80% or less)

## Tax Benefits of Rental Property

### Depreciation
The IRS lets you deduct the value of the building (not land) over 27.5 years. This creates "paper losses" that offset rental income — even if the property appreciates in real life.

### Deductible Expenses
Mortgage interest, property taxes, insurance, repairs, management fees, travel to the property, depreciation.

### Passive Activity Rules
Rental income is "passive." Passive losses can only offset passive income, not W-2 income — unless you qualify as a Real Estate Professional (750+ hours/year in real estate).

Exception: If your AGI is under $100,000, you can deduct up to $25,000 in rental losses against ordinary income.

### 1031 Exchange
Sell a rental property and defer all capital gains taxes by reinvesting in a "like-kind" property within 180 days. Can be done indefinitely, deferring taxes for a lifetime.

## Pros and Cons vs. REITs
Direct real estate offers higher potential returns (leverage + tax benefits) but requires active management, capital, and time. REITs offer liquidity and diversification with no management hassle. Both have a place in a diversified portfolio.
